## 1. Seated drape (skirt, longyi, htamein)

### Idea: the lathe becomes a swept tube along the legs
The cloth is a swept elliptical tube. Its centre line runs on the legs, and its cross-section encloses both legs by construction. Because the enclosure is analytic, the "outside the legs" guarantee does not rest on tuning.

**Centre line (4 nodes, hips-local, in the y-z plane, computed per frame).** Let `a` = thigh pitch and `b` = thigh pitch plus knee bend, from the pose, both legs averaged (`p.legLx`, `p.kneeL`, ...).
- N0 the waist, (y .1, z 0), the same as the lathe top.
- N1 the thigh mid, at hip + .5 up along the thigh.
- N2 the knee, hip + up along the thigh, with a mitre (the outer offset is scaled by 1 / cos(half turn)).
- N3 the hem, down the shin: knee + (lo - .03) along (b). That is a little above the ankle. This replaces the fixed `skirtHem`, so the hem never goes below the foot.

**Cross-section at a node.** Half-width along x: `ax = hipX + sin|legLz| * dist + r + m`, where `r` = .066 (thigh) or .058 (shin) and the margin `m` = .02. Half-thickness along the node's normal `n` (perpendicular to the path in the y-z plane): `rho = r + m + .02`. The ellipse with these axes contains two circles of radius `r` at x = ±hipX with at least 6 mm to spare. At the waist (N0) `ax, rho` blend back to the lathe's own radii.

**Per vertex (precomputed once at build into Float32Arrays `skU`, `skCos`, `skV`):**
- `u` = (top - y) / (top - hem), the arc fraction, so it uses the existing profile height. The node interpolation is piecewise linear in `u` over the four nodes, with the node arcs recomputed each frame from their lengths (6 floats).
- `cosφ` = x / rx(u), the base ring's own x fraction (so the existing vertical stripes stay).
- `v` = z / rz(u), the base ring's own z fraction, signed: front > 0, back < 0.
- Seated position = `C(u) + ax(u) * cosφ * x̂ + rho(u) * v * n̂(u)`.

**Front / back / sides.** From the single formula:
- Front (`v > 0`) lies on the top of the thighs and runs over the knee. With `n` pointing up on the lap and forward on the shin, the front half is the lap on top and the shin curtain at the front.
- Back (`v < 0`) is the underside of the lap (under the thighs) and the cloth behind the calves, the way a skirt tucks under a sitting person.
- Sides: `cosφ` large uses the whole `ax`, so the cloth closes over the outer thighs / knees and no gap shows from the side. The two thighs are one wide lap with the dip described next.
- A small sag between the knees: front vertices with |x| < .05 get `-.018 * (1 - |x| / .05)` along `n` on the lap segment. It reads as cloth, not as a board. It is subtracted from the margin and the check measures it.
- A few cm of "freedom" in `n` at the hem: the hem ring is slightly wider than the shin tube (`rho * 1.15`), so it does not look shrink-wrapped.

### Blending (sitdown / standup, and walking)
`s` ∈ [0,1], the seatedness, from the final pose `p` of the frame, so `sitdown` / `standup` / loop-to-loop crossfades give it for free (they already `mix` the pose): `s = smoothstep(.55, 1.1, min(-p.legLx, -p.legRx))`. Using the *less* bent leg means a walking stride (one leg back) or a pedalling leg never raises it. The lap path sits at thigh pitch -1.45, so squat (similar thigh, shin folded) works through the same formula with `b` from the pose.
- `s = 0`: the current deform, byte for byte (the standing / walking / hitched look must not change; `s` is exactly 0 there, and the branch is skipped, so the cost is the same as today).
- `0 < s < 1`: `pos = lerp(standing deform, seated tube, s)`. Cost is both formulas for those few frames.
- The hold lift (`p.hold`, the hitched longyi) is skipped by `s`: seated people do not hitch.
- `cycle` / `coast`: use the same formula, since the pose measurements show `cycle` thighs near -1.2 and one leg always bent. If the tube looks wrong there I will gate it (Open Q2).

### Cost / no allocation
- Per frame while `s > 0`: 4 nodes x about 12 flops, then per vertex about 25 flops (one `u` lookup in a 4-entry array, one `sin`-free affine formula). The nodes live in a module-level `Float32Array(24)`, no new objects. Standing NPCs pay nothing new.
- Optional skip: a seated NPC's pose is static, so if the 8 pose floats that feed the nodes equal last frame's, skip the whole deform and the `computeVertexNormals`. That lowers the town's cost below today's. (Today it recomputes for everyone every frame.) I will log both numbers.
- `computeVertexNormals` stays as it is, only when the deform ran.
- The geometry's bounding sphere is stale after the deform; I check that the skirt is not frustum-culled wrongly (the mesh is inside the character's `root`, which is not culled by a stale sphere today either, so this is as it is now).

### Other builds / kids
`ax`, `rho` use the leg radii and `hipX`, which do not depend on the build; only the waist blend uses the build's lathe, so `round`, `slim`, `broad` all work. Kids: `legK` .8 scales `up` / `lo` / the hem; the formulas take them from `c.legK`. The knee-length `skirt` (hem at knee level): same tube but N3 is replaced by the knee node plus a little drop, so it covers the thigh, and I check whether it needs the drape at all (it has the same punch-through risk; yes if the check says so).

## 2. Tucked shirt

### Who
- `longyi` (men) and `htamein` -> tucked by default. New opt `o.tuck` (default true for longSkirt, false for the rest) so `?` / cast data can override per character. Shown as a screenshot pair below to confirm with the creator.
- `pants / shorts / overalls / skirt` -> untucked, the hem outside, but nothing may come through.

### Tucked construction (longyi / htamein)
The skirt lathe gets a waist band. Today's profile top is [r .14-.17, y .1] while the torso at that height (spine y .04) is wider than it (rc .18 round, .14 slim, .12 broad), which is why the hem overhangs. Changes:
- The skirt profile gets a new first point above the old one, the band top, at hips y = **.2** (spine y .14), radius `rb = torsoR(.2) + .012 + bendSlack`, then a band point at y = .12 of the same radius, then the existing points. The band is a straight vertical cuff of about 8 cm that wraps the shirt like a folded waistband. The `u` mapping for the seated deform takes `top` = .2.
- The torso lathe's hem changes from the flare [hemR, -.095] to a tucked taper: [rIn, -.07] / [rIn, -.03], where `rIn` = `rb - .03`. That puts the shirt's hem about 4 cm inside the band's cuff, hidden. Above the band the shirt keeps its own shape (rc), so the band overlaps the lower chest like a sash.
- The rows of the torso lathe between y = .06 and .14 (inside the cuff) stay at rc (they already do), so the shirt appears to go into the band.
- The ပုဆိုး knot (`B.longyi[1][0] - .005`, y .06) moves up to the band (y .14) and out to `rb + .005` so it stays in front, on the cuff.
- The hip ball: a skin sphere under the skirt. It is already inside the band. The check includes it.
- Print: `printTex` paints the chest part of the torso texture (UV v near the top). The tuck only changes the lathe below y = .06, so the print is not cut by the band. I will screenshot a "print" shirt tucked, close-up, to confirm. The band covers only spine y < .14, and the print sits at chest level (y .14 .. .28).

### Bend slack
With the spine bending, a point at height h above the spine pivot moves h * sin(rx). The band must contain the tucked shirt in the worst bend, so `bendSlack` and the tuck depth are derived from a measured maximum, not guessed: the check first prints the worst spine pitch / roll / yaw over all loops, one-shots (the poses with the biggest are expected from sit-down lean, the throws, push, cycle, laugh), then I size `bendSlack` so the worst margin is positive. If the needed slack looks fat (more than about .03) I switch to the skinned-hem fallback below.

### Untucked construction (pants, shorts, overalls, skirt)
Static changes first (pure geometry, no per-frame cost):
- The hip ball is lowered and flattened (scale y .75 -> .6), so its top sits at spine y .066, below the chest rows. (For pants the ball is `max(B.hip, .16)` wide in pantsMat; that is what can poke at the slim / broad builds, where `rc` is .12-.14 and the ball is .16.)
- The shirt hem becomes a bell: the hem ring is wider and lower, and the ring 1 (y = -.06) and the point at y = .06 are wide enough that the hem hangs over the ball in every bend (margin and shape from the check).
- For shorts / overalls: the same, and the shorts cylinders' tops (r .09-.1 at the hip) are sampled.

Fallback if a static bell still leaves poses uncovered (Open Q1): **skin the torso's lower rings to the hips.** For torso verts with spine y < 0, a weight `w(y)` (1 at the hem, 0 at y = .06) applies the inverse of the spine's local rotation, so the hem ring stays level with the hips instead of swinging with the spine. This is about 100 verts, only while the spine rotation is not tiny, with module-level scratch, plus `computeVertexNormals` on the torso (cheap). The torso is currently in the outline hull set (never changes shape), so it would need to be taken out of it. This is the more robust way and the more invasive one, so I only do it if needed.

## 3. The geometric checks (`docs/cloth/check.cjs`)
Runs in the bench page via `page.evaluate`, no rendering, so the 1 fps limit does not matter. One in-page loop builds each character with `makeChibi`, sets the pose, steps the animator, calls `updateMatrixWorld(true)` and tests with a `THREE.Raycaster` (the skirt and torso materials are DoubleSide / I set `material.side` for the test only).

**Helper `insideBy(mesh, axisPoint, samplePoint)`:** cast a ray from `axisPoint` (a point known to be inside) through `samplePoint`; the nearest hit distance must be greater than `|sample - axis| + margin`. A miss counts as a failure unless the sample is below the hem (cloth vs leg) or above the band top (tucked), which is legitimately open.

**Check A, cloth vs legs (seated and every pose):**
- Samples: for both thighs and both shins, 8 stations along the bone (capsule axis; hip to knee, knee to ankle) x 12 directions around it on the capsule surface (radius `r`); the axis station is the ray origin.
- Only the bone parts above the hem (y > hem + .02 in hips space) count. Margin **.012 units** (about 1 cm; 1 unit is about .75 m).
- Applies to skirt / longyi / htamein, and to the pants cloth cylinders (hem flare) as a bonus. The hip ball is also a sample: its 24 surface points against the shirt (Check B).

**Check B, top vs bottom:**
- Untucked (pants, shorts, overalls, skirt): samples = the hip ball surface (24 points, the ellipsoid), the top 8 cm of each thigh capsule / shorts cylinder / pants leg (8 points around x 3 heights), and the skirt top ring (28). Tested against the torso mesh from the spine axis, only for samples whose y is above the torso hem and below the chest, with margin **.005**.
- Tucked (longyi, htamein): samples = the torso verts below the band top (the hem rings and the 2 rows above), tested against the skirt mesh from the hips axis at the same height; margin **.01** (the shirt inside the band). Plus the hip ball inside the band.

**Poses:** every `LOOPS` (idle walk jog run dance sit eat vend squat ready push carry cycle coast) at 3 phases, every `ONESHOT` (jump splash squirt shake wave kadaw kick laugh call sitdown standup pick bigthrow) at 6 times through its duration (sitdown / standup mid-way is the drape blend), the `chat` layers (speak / listen over idle and sit), and the loops registered from `tics/*.json` through the same `registerTic` loader `cast.js` uses (stir, sprinkle, peek, listen, refuse, munch, and so on), `load = 'front' / 'head' / 'hand'`, the water tools. About 110 poses.

**Outfits:** 6 bottoms x 3 builds x {adult, kid} x top {tee, yinphone} = 72 characters, plus the 2 elders (`hunch`: Bwa Bwa and Saya Gyi's real opts). About 8,000 pose evaluations, about 1.3M rays, a few minutes of CPU.

The check prints a table (outfit x pose with the worst margin), the top 5 worst, and ALL PASS / FAIL. It also logs the numbers it picked: worst spine bend over all poses, and the maximum `s` jump per frame (the drape should not snap).

**Frame time:** in the same page, 40 characters (a mix of seated / standing / walking), `anim.update(.016)` x 600, ms per call before / after (my "before" is `git stash`-free: I run the same check against `main` through a second worktree path or the committed old file). Headless rendering is too slow to say anything about GPU time, so I log CPU update time; the deform and the `computeVertexNormals` are the only things I change.

**Existing checks:** `docs/day1b/check.cjs` and `docs/mood/check.cjs` must still ALL PASS; console 0 on the bench page and on `yangon.html`.

## Results (built, review 1 applied)

Review 1 asked for a snug navel-height waist band and a longer seated curtain. Applied; the checks gained asserts for both.

### What changed in `chibi.js`
- **Seated drape.** `seatNodes()` builds four nodes (waist, hip root, knee, hem) each frame from the thigh pitch and knee bend; every skirt vertex keeps its arc fraction `u` and ring fractions (precomputed) and is placed on an ellipse swept down them (front = lap and shin curtain, back = under the thighs and behind the calves). The hem hangs straight down from the knee to **3 cm above the ankle** (adult 3.0 cm, kid 2.4 cm in `sit`), so a squat's folded shins are inside it. Blend `s = smoothstep(min(-legLx, -legRx), .55, 1.1)`: sitdown / standup crossfade for free, a walking stride never triggers it; a seated pose that has not changed skips the deform. The knee-length skirt gets a square hem at the knee. The hem border texture now follows the cloth length (`uv.y = u`), so the yellow border sits at the hem, not at the knees, when seated.
- **Tucked shirt, review 1.** The band top is at the **navel (hips y 0)**, the old untucked hem was at -.035. The band is a snug 5 cm cuff (radius = hip ball x 1.05 + 1.4 cm) over the shirt, whose waist is 5 to 8 mm inside it (a cone from the band up to the chest row; the five rows below the chest keep their UV rows, so the print is not stretched: `after-tuck-closeup.jpg`). The visible shirt is **89%** of the torso (shoulder line to old hem; asked >= 60%). The standing swing leaves the cuff alone (`hTop`), so the rim stays snug when the legs swing.
